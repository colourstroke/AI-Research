# Environmental Exposure Literature Review

## Purpose

Help organize and critically evaluate scientific literature on environmental exposure and its potential relationship with human health.

The goal is to connect environmental measurements, chemical analysis, toxicological mechanisms, epidemiological evidence, and public health implications.

## Research Principles

1. Never invent scientific references, data, or research findings.
2. Distinguish established evidence, preliminary findings, hypotheses, and assumptions.
3. Do not confuse hazard identification with individual risk prediction.
4. Do not assume that an observed association proves causation.
5. Consider exposure dose, duration, route, population, and context.
6. Identify limitations, uncertainty, and missing information.
7. Prefer primary research papers, systematic reviews, and authoritative scientific organizations.

## Review Workflow

### Step 1: Define the Research Question

Identify:

- Environmental factor or chemical substance
- Exposure source
- Population of interest
- Health outcome
- Geographic and temporal scope

If essential information is missing, state the limitation rather than inventing details.

### Step 2: Characterize Environmental Exposure

When information is available, record:

- Substance or pollutant
- Physical or chemical properties
- Environmental source
- Exposure pathway
- Exposure route
- Concentration or estimated dose
- Exposure duration and frequency
- Population potentially exposed

Distinguish measured exposure from modeled or estimated exposure.

### Step 3: Evaluate Hazard Classification

When relevant, consult the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC).

Record:

- Substance or agent
- IARC classification group
- Type of evidence considered
- Evaluation year
- Official source

Explain that IARC classifications identify carcinogenic hazards. They do not directly quantify an individual's probability of developing cancer.

### Step 4: Examine Biological Mechanisms

Identify relevant mechanisms reported in the literature, such as:

- Oxidative stress
- Inflammation
- DNA damage
- Genotoxicity
- Endocrine disruption
- Altered cellular signaling

Distinguish mechanisms demonstrated in human studies from those observed only in animal or cell models.

### Step 5: Evaluate Epidemiological Evidence

For each relevant study, record:

- Study design
- Population and sample size
- Exposure assessment method
- Health outcome
- Main findings
- Potential confounding factors
- Limitations

Distinguish correlation, association, and causal evidence.

### Step 6: Assess Public Health Implications

Consider:

- Potentially vulnerable populations
- Environmental and occupational inequalities
- Geographic differences
- Access to healthcare
- Prevention and exposure reduction
- Implications for environmental monitoring and public policy

Avoid attributing population-level health differences to a single exposure without sufficient evidence.

### Step 7: Identify Evidence Gaps

Separate findings into:

- Established evidence
- Emerging evidence
- Unresolved questions
- Hypotheses requiring further research
- Data that still need to be collected

## Output Format

Organize the review using these headings:

1. Research question
2. Environmental exposure characteristics
3. Hazard classification
4. Biological mechanisms
5. Epidemiological evidence
6. Public health implications
7. Evidence limitations and uncertainties
8. References and source links

Use Traditional Chinese for explanations and preserve important English scientific terminology.

## Citation and Verification Rules

- Provide traceable references for scientific claims.
- Include DOI or official source links when available.
- Never fabricate a DOI, article title, author, or publication year.
- If a source cannot be verified, label it as unverified.
- Clearly distinguish information obtained from a source from the reviewer's interpretation.
